How to read this page

{CA-1:key} this row's own ID {P-1} an ID that points to another row

Click any ID to light it up everywhere on the page. A bar at the bottom lists where it's created and where it's used. That's the fastest way to see a relationship.

  • All names, numbers and codes are made up. Real IDs are long UUIDs; short ones are easier to follow.
  • To keep tables readable, the page leaves out workspace_id, recorded_at, updated_at and empty since / until dates. The history rows' row_snapshot holds the full row as JSON.

The map

Three entities own the tables. Reference data is used by them and belongs to none of them. Each line points from the row holding the ID to the row it refers to.

Lines from private_anchor to contact, and from creditor_account to operated_party, are left off the drawing to keep it readable. They're in the full list near the end.

0

Before ABC exists

Set up in advance. Vendor creation only reads these rows.

Ledger {2101} is the control account. The groups above it give the hierarchy through parent_account_code. Category {GOODS_MSME} names that ledger, so nobody chooses a ledger on the form. The group names here are examples until Finance supplies the real ones.

Our company is also a party ({P-OUR}), and Ravi and Meena are existing contacts, because the request has to point to them.

1

Ravi fills in and submits the form

Accounts Executive · SaveVendorRequest, then SubmitVendorRequest

Procurement hands Ravi the paper form, and Ravi types it in. If the paper can't be used, Ravi returns it with a reason: the stage becomes returned, return_reason is filled in, and no decision happens. In this example Ravi submits it.

Everything Ravi types stays on the request. No vendor, party or accounting row exists yet. The right-hand column shows where each value will go if Meena approves.

The extra GSTINs are a list inside one column, additional_gst_registrations, because they're saved and read with the form and nothing refers to them separately. Each version of the request gets its own history row, which points to {R-101}.

2

Meena decides

Accounts Manager · DecideVendorRequest
decisionstage becomeswhat else happens
approveactiveSteps 3, 4 and 5 run. Followed below.
rejectrejectedA reason is required. The request stays as a record and no vendor is created.
send_backdraftA reason is required. Ravi corrects the form and submits it again.

The person who submitted a request can't also decide it, because of the rule decided_by <> submitted_by. Here Ravi submitted and Meena decides, so it passes.

One transaction: steps 3, 4 and 5 are saved together. If anything fails, nothing is saved and the request stays submitted.

3

Parties records who ABC is

RegisterSupplier · 28 rows in 10 tables

Parties owns identity: the legal name, tax numbers, addresses and the people we deal with. The action returns ABC's IDs to the next step.

How these rows connect

  • Shared ID: {P-1} and {C-1} are created in private_anchor first, then the same value becomes the party_id and the contact_id.
  • Many rows for one party: {ID-1} to {ID-4}, {LOC-1}, {LOC-2} and {NM-1} all point to {P-1}.
  • A vendor of which company: {PS-1} says ABC ({P-1}) supplies our company ({OP-1}), under the name {NM-1}.
  • Contact person: a person contact has no party row, so {AS-1} links Asha ({C-1}) to ABC ({P-1}). Asha's mobile and email are {CP-1} and {CP-2}.
  • No foreign key from identifiers to locations: {ID-3} and {LOC-2} are connected only by having the same state, IN-TN. Reference table gst_state_code is how GST code 33 became IN-TN.
4

Accounting opens ABC's account

OpenCreditorAccount · 5 rows in 4 tables

This is the subledger row. The action reads category {GOODS_MSME}, finds ledger {2101} and copies it onto the account. The chart of accounts gets no new row.

How these rows connect

  • Into Parties: {CA-1} points to {P-1}, {OP-1} and {PS-1}. Finance stores the ID and leaves the identity in Parties.
  • Into the chart: {CA-1} points to ledger {2101}, so ABC rolls up under Sundry Creditors.
  • Bank: {CB-1} belongs to {CA-1}. The account number from the request is copied here.
  • GST: {TR-1} and {TR-2} each link {CA-1} to a GSTIN row. The number stays in Parties, while Finance keeps the type and which one is the default.
  • Only once: UNIQUE (workspace_id, operated_party_id, party_id) stops ABC getting a second account in the same company.
5

The request is finished

Back in DecideVendorRequest · 1 new history row and 1 updated request

The request now points to what it created. Highlighted cells are the columns that changed.

{R-101} and {CA-1} now point to each other. creditor_account.opened_from_request_id is a declared foreign key, and vendor_request.creditor_account_id is a plain pointer, because two foreign keys would make a circle.

What was and wasn't created

GroupTableNew rows

Not created: no ledger row, no journal entry and no balance column. ABC's balance is zero until its first bill. That later bill line will carry ledger {2101} and vendor {CA-1}; the bill task will design that table.

What to keep in mind

  • Seed rows still missing: supplier, IN_UDYAM and vendor_contact are used above, but they still need seeding in Parties (gaps 2, 4 and 5).
  • Assumed values: record_type names in pc.record_history, other than party, are my assumption. The per-company chart question (Q19) is also still open and could change the ledger key.
  • Outbox: the model writes events to it at approval. It's left out here because the gaps document marks it as not needed for this flow.
