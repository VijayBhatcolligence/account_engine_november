Customer Creation Walkthrough

How a customer gets created

Follow one customer, Deccan Fresh, from the paper form Sales hands over to the active customer account. It has three GSTINs in three states. Every table is filled with the same sample data, so you can see exactly how the rows connect. This is the customer twin of the vendor walkthrough: same entities, same names, same flow, plus the extras a customer needs.

How to read this page

{DA-1:key} this row's own ID {P-1} an ID that points to another row

Click any ID to light it up everywhere on the page. A bar at the bottom lists where it's created and where it's used.

  • Deccan Fresh, its numbers and codes are made up (the same as the app's sample form CRF-0021). Real IDs are long UUIDs.
  • Ledger codes are the CA's (Indian GAAP). Under Ind AS the same ledger is 110010: the framework is still to decide (gap G4).
  • The page leaves out workspace_id, recorded_at, updated_at and empty since / until dates. History rows' row_snapshot holds the full row as JSON.

The map

Three entities own the tables: CustomerRequest (what was typed), DebtorAccount (the customer in our books) and Party (who the customer is, in Business Core). Reference data is used by them and belongs to none. Each line points from the row holding the ID to the row it refers to.

Left off the drawing to keep it readable: billing location → its tax registration, credit limit → the request, and every *_contact_id → pc.contact. They're in the full list near the end.

0

Before Deccan exists

Set up in advance. Customer creation only reads these rows.

Ledger {107010} is the control account; the groups above it give the hierarchy through parent_account_code. Category {DOMESTIC_B2B_REGULAR} names that ledger, so nobody picks a ledger on the form. It also carries the CA's GST, TCS and credit-period words that the form shows as "System suggestions".

Our company is also a party ({P-OUR}). Arun, Teja, Rajan, Farid and Venkat are existing contacts, because the request and its approvals point to them.

1

Teja types the form and submits it

Accounts Executive · SaveCustomerRequest, then SubmitCustomerRequest

Arun hands Teja the Customer Master onboarding form (sections A–F and B1), and Teja types it in. If the paper can't be used, Teja returns it to Arun with a reason: the stage becomes returned, return_reason is filled in, and nobody approves anything. Here Teja submits it.

Everything Teja types stays on the request. No customer, party or accounting row exists yet. The right-hand column shows where each value goes once all three parts are approved.

The two extra GSTINs are a list inside one column, additional_gst_registrations, as on the vendor side: they're saved and read with the form, and nothing refers to them separately. Submit opens one approval row per part ({AP-1}, {AP-2}, {AP-3}), all for submission 1.

2

Three checkers decide, each their part

Farid, Venkat, then Rajan · DecideCustomerRequestPart

The person who submitted can't approve any part: approver_contact_id <> submitted_by_contact_id. Teja submitted; Farid, Venkat and Rajan decide, so it passes. Rajan's approval is the last one.

One transaction, on Rajan's approval: steps 3, 4 and 5 are saved together. If anything fails, nothing is saved and the request stays submitted.

3

Parties records who Deccan is

RegisterCustomer · 28 rows in 10 tables

Parties owns identity: the legal name, PAN and every GSTIN, the places, and the person we deal with. The action returns Deccan's IDs to the next step.

How these rows connect

  • Shared ID: {P-1} and {C-1} are created in private_anchor first; the same value becomes the party_id and the contact_id.
  • Three GSTINs, one party: {ID-2}, {ID-3} and {ID-4} all point to {P-1}. One PAN ({ID-1}) is inside all three.
  • Places: {LOC-1} is the registered office. {LOC-2} is the Chennai branch, from its B1 row. Kochi gave no address, so it has no place yet.
  • A customer of which company: {PS-1} says Deccan ({P-1}) is a customer of our company ({OP-1}), under the name {NM-1}.
  • No foreign key from GSTINs to places: P&C links a GSTIN to the party, not to a place. Which place is billed under which GSTIN is Finance's, in step 4.
4

Accounting opens Deccan's account

OpenDebtorAccount · 9 rows in 6 tables

This is the AR sub-ledger row. The action reads category {DOMESTIC_B2B_REGULAR}, finds ledger {107010} and copies it onto the account. The chart of accounts gets no new row. The customer gets its own code, C000001, never built from the ledger code.

How these rows connect

  • Into Parties: {DA-1} points to {P-1}, {OP-1} and {PS-1}. Finance stores the ID and leaves the identity in Parties.
  • Into the chart: {DA-1} points to ledger {107010}, so Deccan rolls up under Trade Receivables.
  • GST: {TR-1}, {TR-2} and {TR-3} each link {DA-1} to one GSTIN row. {TR-1} is the default billing GSTIN.
  • Where we bill: {BL-1} bills {LOC-1} under {TR-1}; {BL-2} bills {LOC-2} under {TR-2}. {TR-3} (Kochi) has no billing location yet: it is added when the address comes.
  • Credit: {CL-1} is the current limit, approved by Farid. A new limit later adds a row and closes this one.
  • Only once: UNIQUE (workspace_id, operated_party_id, party_id): one account for Deccan in our company, however many GSTINs.
  • To the engine: {EV-1} tells the accounting engine the account exists. The engine logs it as seen and posts nothing.
5

The request is finished

Back in DecideCustomerRequestPart · 1 new history row and 1 updated request

The request now points to what it created. Highlighted cells are the columns that changed.

{R-101} and {DA-1} now point to each other. debtor_account.opened_from_request_id is a declared foreign key, and customer_request.debtor_account_id is a plain pointer, because two foreign keys would make a circle.

What was and wasn't created

GroupTableNew rows

Not created: no ledger row, no journal entry and no balance column. Deccan's balance is zero until its first invoice. That invoice line will carry ledger {107010}, account {DA-1} and the billing location it bills; the invoice task designs that table. Also updated, not new: {AP-1} (Rajan's approval) and {R-101}.

    What to keep in mind

    • Changed from rev 2: the company key is now operated_party_id, as on the vendor side (rev 2's fin.book is gone; more books per company is task S1). customer_account is now debtor_account. customer_branch is now debtor_tax_registration plus debtor_billing_location. The reactions and the hold stage give way to one transaction.
    • Business Core must seed: standing type customer, capacity customer_contact, and the legal forms beyond IN_PVT_LTD (partnership, LLP, HUF, trust / society, government, foreign). Customer groups use party_relationship: its type is still to agree (gap G5).
    • App vs schema: the app lets Teja edit the ledger, GST and TCS suggestions, but here the category decides them, as on the vendor side. Whether the CA wants per-customer overrides is open. The app's single LUT field is split here into number and valid-until; the collection owner is a contact here, text in the app.
    • The sales invoice: its receivable codes don't match the CA's chart (Export & SEZ uses 107030, which is Government in the chart; Other uses 107140, which is Provision for Doubtful Debts). Logged for the invoice task.
    • Still to bring up to date: the Customer Backend Walkthrough still describes the older reaction and hold flow (gap G11).
